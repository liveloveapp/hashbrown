# Paint atc's airspace with the chart's own two inks

The current FAA VFR sectional uses two aeronautical spot inks: a cyan-leaning **blue (PMS 307U)** and a magenta that on the chart itself prints as a **dark maroon (PMS 259U)**. Black covers the rest. What a feature looks like on the sectional comes from three things: which of those inks it uses, a few screen percentages, and a small set of print line weights. The weights at 1:500,000 are **0.060" for Class B and C, 0.020" dashed for Class D and Class E surface, a 0.1875" vignette for Class E floors, and a 0.100" tick "comb" for special use airspace**. Measured on the 3 September 2026 GeoTIFFs, solid blue is **#086CA8** and solid magenta is **#76334F**. Class B is a 45% blue screen (**#3288B5**) and Class C a 45% magenta screen (**#A6768E**). These values differ sharply from the violet-looking colours in the FAA's own user-guide PDF, and atc should follow the chart, not the PDF. Leaflet already draws stroke weights and dash arrays in screen pixels. So the most faithful approach is to fix every line at its print weight converted at 96 px per inch, which coincides with true chart scale at about zoom 9.7 over Oregon, and thin it at overview zooms. The vignettes and comb ticks can both be built from stacked or dashed strokes clipped to the polygon interior, with no gradients and no new dependency; that also keeps atc's design-rules test green. Ink #0d0d0d already matches the chart's black layer. Chart black covers TRSAs, MTRs, VFR waypoints and airport-diagram runways, so ink stays the colour for everything the FAA does not paint blue or magenta. The serious conflict is with cobalt. The AWC's MVFR blue **#0000EE** has almost the same luminance as atc's **#002fa7** (contrast ratio about 1.1:1). Chart blue is only about 1.9:1 from cobalt. Selection therefore has to be shown by form (a casing ring, extra weight, dimming everything else), never by recolouring a chart feature cobalt. Finally, several assumptions in the brief are wrong on the current chart. **Redmond (KRDM) is Class D with a part-time tower, Bend (KBDN) is non-towered inside a Class E surface area, and Medford (KMFR) is charted Class D.**

Basis tags used throughout: **[M]** is measured from the FAA GeoTIFF palette or pixels, **[M-pdf]** is measured from the FAA user guide PDF as rendered (a weaker, secondary measurement), **[S]** is stated in an FAA specification or guide, and **[E]** is an estimate or recommendation of this report.

## The chart prints maroon and cyan-blue, not the violet of the user guide

The production specification names the separations, and the colour assignment follows from them. **Blue** carries towered airports and their data, Class B, Class D, Class E at 1,200 ft AGL and above, prohibited/restricted/warning areas, Victor airways, VHF navaids and maximum elevation figures. **Magenta** carries non-towered airports, LF/MF navaids, MOAs and alert areas, Class C, Class E surface and 700 ft AGL, Mode C, ADIZ and VFR checkpoint flags. **Black** carries MTRs, TRSAs and VFR waypoints ([IAC 2 §3.4.5–3.4.7](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/flight_info/aeronav/iac/media/IAC2/IAC-2-20JUL2026-complete.pdf)). The chart legend states the airport rule plainly: towered airports are blue, all others magenta, and the data block takes the symbol's colour ([CUG p. 23](https://aeronav.faa.gov/user_guide/cug-complete_20260709.pdf); [IAC 2 §3.9.2.6](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/flight_info/aeronav/iac/media/IAC2/IAC-2-20JUL2026-complete.pdf)).

The two sources disagree on ink numbers. The **1 August 2023 IAC 2 edition's Table 4.1 assigns PMS 307U to blue and PMS 259U to magenta**. The same table gives the screens: Class B and Class C at 45%/45°/200L, VHF airways at 22%, LF/MF airways at 22% magenta ([IAC 2, 2023, Table 4.1](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/flight_info/aeronav/iac/media/IAC2/IAC-2-1AUG2023-complete.pdf)) [S]. A full-text search of the **20 July 2026 edition found no Pantone, PMS or CMYK numbers at all**; it cites only the Pantone Matching System as a reference standard ([IAC 2, 2026 §1.3.2](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/flight_info/aeronav/iac/media/IAC2/IAC-2-20JUL2026-complete.pdf)). Treat the 307U/259U assignment as spec as of 2023, unconfirmed for 2026. The one third-party conversion found puts 307U at about #0072A6, close to the measured blue, but that aggregator is low-confidence ([Hextoral](https://hextoral.com/hex-color/3373A9/pantone/)).

The measured values are the ones to build on. The Klamath Falls and Seattle GeoTIFFs for the cycle effective 3 Sep 2026 are 8-bit palette images at 300 dpi, so their colours can be read directly with no JPEG noise ([Klamath Falls](https://aeronav.faa.gov/visual/09-03-2026/sectional-files/Klamath_Falls.zip); [Seattle](https://aeronav.faa.gov/visual/09-03-2026/sectional-files/Seattle.zip)) [M]. On white legend paper, solid blue reads **#086CA8** and solid magenta **#76334F**, with a darker magenta edge of **#691735** [M]. Over the tan terrain tint the same inks overprint like a multiply blend. Blue becomes teal **#086972/#086A8E** around Redmond, and Class B over city yellow turns olive green, **#3F8449** [M]. The FAA's ArcGIS tile service agrees within JPEG noise, with dark-blue clusters near #086870 and dark-magenta near #803040. That service stops at z12, which is the GeoTIFF upsampled about 1.5× ([VFR_Sectional MapServer](https://tiles.arcgis.com/tiles/ssFJjBXIUyZDrSYZ/arcgis/rest/services/VFR_Sectional/MapServer)) [M].

The user guide PDF tells a different colour story. Rasterising its legend art gives a towered-airport blue of **#3884C4–#3A87C6** and a magenta of **#703884–#9C579F**, a clear violet ([CUG pp. 24–30](https://aeronav.faa.gov/user_guide/cug-complete_20260709.pdf)) [M-pdf]. That is the colour of a PDF, not of the chart pilots fly with. The reference crops below show maroon airport symbols and dashes at Bend. The IAC itself warns that digital display may "vary slightly in appearance" ([IAC 2, ch. 1](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/flight_info/aeronav/iac/media/IAC2/IAC-2-20JUL2026-complete.pdf)). **atc should take the GeoTIFF values and treat the PDF values as a fallback only.** Screened bands have to be taken as measured, not computed. A naive 45% tint of #086CA8 on white would be about #90C0DC, yet Class B measures #3288B5 [M].

## Line weights convert to screen pixels at zoom 9.7

The IAC gives every airspace line weight in print inches at 1:500,000 ([IAC 2 App. 7](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/flight_info/aeronav/iac/media/IAC2/IAC-2-20JUL2026-complete.pdf)) [S]. The GeoTIFF measurements fall within about 5% of spec [M]:

- The KPDX Class C ring measures **19–20 px (1.6 mm)** against a 0.060" (1.52 mm) spec.
- A KSEA Class B internal line measures **12–13 px (1.05 mm)** against 0.040".
- The KRDM Class D ring measures a **6 px mode (≈0.5 mm)** against 0.020".
- The 700 ft vignette east of Bend measures **≈63 px (5.3 mm)** against 0.1875" (4.76 mm).

Class D and Class E surface dashes have no numeric spec. On the chart they measure **24–24.5 px dash and 6–7 px gap, that is 2.05 mm on and 0.55 mm off**, which is **4.0 : 1.1 in units of line width** [M]. The estimate from the PDF legend (dash 3.5–4×, gap 1.4×) is close but coarser [M-pdf]. The only dimensioned dash patterns in the spec belong to national security areas and national-security TFRs: 0.150" dash, 0.100" space, 0.060" line ([IAC 2 App. 7 p. A-32](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/flight_info/aeronav/iac/media/IAC2/IAC-2-20JUL2026-complete.pdf)) [S].

There are two ways to put these weights on a slippy map. **Geographic scaling** multiplies chart millimetres by 500 to get ground metres and divides by Web Mercator metres per pixel. At 44°N that is about 112,607 / 2^z m/px: 440 at z8, 110 at z10, 27.5 at z12 [E, arithmetic]. **Fixed screen weights** convert print inches at 96 CSS px per inch. The two approaches agree where 1 CSS px equals 0.2646 mm × 500 = 132 m of ground, which is **zoom ≈ 9.7 over central Oregon** [E, arithmetic].

Below that zoom, geographic weights shrink a Class D line to a sub-pixel hairline. Above it, they grow a Class C ring to 28 px at z12. Neither matches how pilots read a chart: paper never zooms, and an EFB's vector layer holds its weights constant. Leaflet's `weight` and `dashArray` are already in screen pixels ([Leaflet Path options](https://leafletjs.com/reference.html#path)). **The recommendation is fixed print-equivalent weights at z ≥ 9, scaled by about 0.6 at z ≤ 8, with the decorative bands (vignette, comb ticks, crenellated floor lines) dropped at z ≤ 7 [E].** atc already treats z ≤ 6 as low zoom for markers (`LOW_ZOOM` in `examples/atc/shared/src/map/airspace-map.ts`).

Two Leaflet details decide whether the dashes read correctly. First, Leaflet's default `lineCap` is `round`, which adds a half-width cap to both ends of every dash. At weight 2 with a 2.2 px gap that closes the gap completely, so every dashed or ticked layer must set `lineCap: 'butt'` ([Leaflet Path options](https://leafletjs.com/reference.html#path)) [E]. Second, the true comb pitch of 2.4 px with 0.7 px ticks aliases into a grey smear on screen. The measured note flagged the same density problem at z10. Opening the pitch to about 3 px with 1 px ticks keeps the comb legible [E].

## Labels follow fixed FAA grammars that atc can render in its own two fonts

**Class B and Class C altitude labels** stack the ceiling over the floor with a horizontal bar between them, in hundreds of feet MSL with the last two zeros dropped ([CUG p. 16](https://aeronav.faa.gov/user_guide/cug-complete_20260709.pdf); [IAC 2 §3.9.4.1–2](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/flight_info/aeronav/iac/media/IAC2/IAC-2-20JUL2026-complete.pdf)) [S]. "SFC" means surface. A Class B floor prefixed "+" means "upward from above". A Class C ceiling of "T" means up to but not including the overlying Class B floor. Class B figures are **solid** blue, not the screened band colour; Class C figures are solid magenta. The chart sets them in 8–24 pt Helvetica Condensed Bold, sized to the sector, with leader lines when a label will not fit. The "70/40" in the brief should therefore render on the map as a stacked fraction. The slash form belongs only in running text such as a detail card [E].

**Class D ceilings** sit in a dashed box. On the chart this box is four corner brackets with each side broken in the middle, visible as the "56" box beside Redmond in the reference crop and the "40" box in the legend. A leading minus, as in "-29", means from the surface up to but not including that altitude ([IAC 2 §3.9.4.3](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/flight_info/aeronav/iac/media/IAC2/IAC-2-20JUL2026-complete.pdf); [CUG pp. 16, 27](https://aeronav.faa.gov/user_guide/cug-complete_20260709.pdf)) [S]. A Class E surface area with a ceiling uses the same box. Its colour is not stated, and magenta to match the E dashes is the reasonable reading [E].

The box can be drawn exactly as an inline SVG square of side _L_ with `stroke-dasharray: 0.65L 0.35L` and `stroke-dashoffset: 0.325L`. That centres a dash on every corner [E]. CSS `border: dashed` cannot do this, because it puts gaps at the corners.

**Class E floor changes** that no vignette marks are drawn as a blue crenellated line, with rectangles alternating on either side of the centreline. The floor value sits on each side ("2400 MSL" over "4500 MSL" in the legend; the CUG's inline example uses "2400 AGL" / "4500 MSL"). Ceilings below 18,000 ft read "CEILING 14,000 MSL" along the same line. Class E at 14,500 ft and above is not charted ([CUG p. 17](https://aeronav.faa.gov/user_guide/cug-complete_20260709.pdf); [IAC 2 §3.9.4.4](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/flight_info/aeronav/iac/media/IAC2/IAC-2-20JUL2026-complete.pdf)) [S]. The symbol has no dimensions in either document.

**Airspace names** go outside the boundary near its north point, in 9 pt bold caps: "SEATTLE CLASS B" in blue, a Class C name in magenta, TRSA names in black ([IAC 2 App. 7 pp. A-26, A-28](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/flight_info/aeronav/iac/media/IAC2/IAC-2-20JUL2026-complete.pdf)) [S]. SUA labels use the letter-number designator ("R-4801", "W-93") or a name ("JUNIPER B MOA"), placed inside or just outside the area. SUAs with floors at FL180 or above are omitted ([IAC 2 §3.9.4.10](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/flight_info/aeronav/iac/media/IAC2/IAC-2-20JUL2026-complete.pdf)) [S].

**The airport data block** sits under the symbol in the symbol's colour. Its lines, top to bottom, are:

1. FSS and NO SVFR.
2. NAME (IDENT); a boxed name marks FAR 93.
3. "CT - freq", with ★ for part-time and Ⓒ after whichever frequency is the CTAF, then ATIS.
4. Elevation, lighting (L, \*L or -), runway length in hundreds of feet, and UNICOM.
5. RP runways, then VFR Advsy, then AOE.

"(Pvt)" marks private fields. A dash replaces missing data, and zero is slashed ([CUG pp. 15, 24](https://aeronav.faa.gov/user_guide/cug-complete_20260709.pdf); [IAC 2 §3.9.2.4–3.9.2.5](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/flight_info/aeronav/iac/media/IAC2/IAC-2-20JUL2026-complete.pdf)) [S]. The chart sets it in two weights. Names and tags are 6.5 pt medium caps; tower and ATIS figures are 7–7.5 pt condensed bold; elevation and UNICOM are 5.5 pt italic ([IAC 2 App. 5 p. A-22](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/flight_info/aeronav/iac/media/IAC2/IAC-2-20JUL2026-complete.pdf)) [S].

atc's design rules allow only Hanken Grotesk and JetBrains Mono, and they forbid `text-transform: uppercase` and positive letter spacing (`examples/atc/shared/src/design-rules.test.ts`). The faithful mapping keeps the chart's two-weight hierarchy inside those limits [E]:

- Names in Hanken Grotesk 600 at about 11 px, with capitals in the data strings themselves. FAA data is already upper case.
- Frequencies and Class B/C/D altitude figures in JetBrains Mono 700 with tabular figures, which atc already uses for numbers.
- Elevation and UNICOM in JetBrains Mono italic at about 10 px.

Mode C's spaced-caps arc label cannot use letter spacing under those rules. Place "MODE C & ADS-B OUT" and "30 NM" as plain caps on an SVG `textPath` instead [E].

## Symbols: airports by tower, runway and fuel; navaids by family

No FAA document dimensions the airport circle, its ticks, or the navaid glyphs. Every pixel size below is therefore an estimate [E]. The geometry rules are spec [S]: shape encodes runway, colour encodes tower, ticks encode fuel ([CUG pp. 14–15, 23](https://aeronav.faa.gov/user_guide/cug-complete_20260709.pdf); [IAC 2 §3.9.2.3, App. 5](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/flight_info/aeronav/iac/media/IAC2/IAC-2-20JUL2026-complete.pdf)). The 8,069 ft threshold exists because runway lengths are charted in hundreds of feet rounded at 70: 8,070 ft becomes "81" ([CUG p. 15](https://aeronav.faa.gov/user_guide/cug-complete_20260709.pdf)).

Fixes split by chart type. On the sectional, named intersections are a blue X with arrows pointing at the defining navaids (magenta for LF/MF). VFR checkpoints are a magenta flag with the name underlined. **VFR waypoints are a black concave four-point star labelled "VPxxx"**, and RNAV waypoints are a smaller blue star ([CUG pp. 32–33](https://aeronav.faa.gov/user_guide/cug-complete_20260709.pdf)) [S]. Filled (compulsory) and open (non-compulsory) triangles belong to IFR enroute charts, in black for VHF and brown for LF/MF ([CUG p. 82](https://aeronav.faa.gov/user_guide/cug-complete_20260709.pdf)) [S]. If atc ever plots IFR fixes, ink triangles follow the IFR convention and stay out of the chart's two inks [E].

Airways on the sectional are bands, not lines. Victor and T routes are a light blue band (**#A6C8E8** [M], a 22% screen). The ID is in solid blue inside the band, with a radial arrow and a boxed total mileage; no MEAs are shown. LF/MF and joint routes are a screened magenta band, and Class G routes are a dashed magenta band ([CUG p. 28](https://aeronav.faa.gov/user_guide/cug-complete_20260709.pdf)) [S].

Airport diagrams follow a separate, monochrome grammar. The d-TPP legend (sheet 26134) draws hard-surface runways solid black and taxiways and aprons flat light grey (**#CFCFCF** [M-pdf]). Stopways and overruns are diagonal-hatched, closed runways get an X at each end, and **hot spots are the only colour: brown #955101** [M-pdf], as an open ellipse leadered to an "HS 1" box ([CUG pp. 124, 132](https://aeronav.faa.gov/user_guide/cug-complete_20260709.pdf)). For the airport overlays proposed in earlier work, that maps onto atc almost unchanged: runways in ink, pavement in a pale grey, hot spots brown, and no blue or magenta anywhere.

## Vignettes and comb ticks fall out of clipped strokes, with no gradients

The FAA describes the Class E vignette as a 0.1875" graded band. Magenta marks a 700 ft AGL floor. Blue marks a floor of 1,200 ft or higher that abuts Class G ([CUG p. 17](https://aeronav.faa.gov/user_guide/cug-complete_20260709.pdf); [IAC 2 App. 7 p. A-27](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/flight_info/aeronav/iac/media/IAC2/IAC-2-20JUL2026-complete.pdf)) [S]. On the chart the band is about ten discrete tint steps, not a smooth ramp [M-pdf]. The measured legend ramp runs **#E8D8C9 → #D2B7C8 → #C997AA → #A6768E** for magenta and **#8BB9D4 → #77B1CE → #5FA1C4** for blue, with a darker blue edge of **#4197BC** [M].

Neither FAA document says which side of the boundary the band lies on. Pilot sources agree that magenta fades inward into the 700 ft area. They describe the blue band's "hard side" as facing the higher-floor Class G and its fuzzy side as where 1,200 ft Class E begins, though one thread disagrees about the blue ([PilotsOfAmerica](https://pilotsofamerica.com/community/threads/understanding-airspace-on-a-sectional-chart.146453/unread?new=1); [AskACFI](https://askacfi.com/?p=25374); [PilotsOfAmerica, Class G and E](https://pilotsofamerica.com/community/threads/class-g-and-e-airspace.82228/page-2)). The best-supported convention is that the **band fades toward the side with the lower Class E floor**. That is secondary-source evidence, not spec.

A CSS or SVG `linearGradient` cannot follow a curved polygon edge. atc's design rules also reject any `gradient(` in its styles and app sources. The chart's own stepped look suggests a better method [E]:

1. Give the airspace polygon an `id` on its Leaflet path (`layer.getElement()`).
2. Add a `<clipPath>` containing `<use href="#that-id">` to the same SVG renderer's `<defs>`. Because `use` references the live path, the clip follows Leaflet's redraws on pan and zoom with no extra code.
3. Draw _n_ ≈ 6 copies of the same ring with `fill: false`, `interactive: false`, weights `2·band·i/n` for i = 1…n, each at about 25% opacity in the vignette colour.
4. Set each copy's `clip-path` to that clip, so only the inner half of each centred stroke survives.

Overlapping copies sum to roughly 82% opacity at the boundary and step down to 25% at the band's inner edge. That is the chart's stepped vignette, built only from strokes. For the blue band, clip to the complement where the data puts the lower floor outside the polygon: a large rectangle plus the polygon path with `clip-rule: evenodd`.

The SUA comb uses the same clip. Draw one extra ring with `weight = 2 × band`, `dashArray = "1 2"` and `lineCap: 'butt'`. Each dash on a wide stroke renders as a short rectangle perpendicular to the boundary, which is exactly a comb tick. Clipping leaves a band of ticks on the inside, and a separate 1.5 px solid ring supplies the baseline [E].

The crenellated Class E floor-change line is two such dashed strokes, one clipped inside and one outside, with the outside copy's `dashOffset` set to half a period [E].

On the canvas renderer, the equivalent is a small `L.Canvas` subclass. Its polygon update calls `ctx.save()`, traces the ring, calls `ctx.clip()`, strokes the copies with `setLineDash`, then calls `ctx.restore()`. That reaches into Leaflet's private `_updatePoly`, so SVG is the cleaner default. atc already creates a dedicated `L.SVG` renderer for its area ring, and the regional airspace set is modest [E].

Two further effects bring the overlay close to the paper look. First, put chart layers in their own pane with `mix-blend-mode: multiply`, which the design rules allow. Overlapping Class C and vignette bands then darken each other the way overprinted inks do, which reproduces the measured "olive over yellow" behaviour [M; E]. Second, atc's greyscale, slightly brightened OSM tiles (`filter: grayscale(1) contrast(0.92) brightness(1.04)` in `map.css`) already sit near the chart's white paper. The "on white" measured values are therefore the right ones; the teal "on tan" values are not [E].

## Highlight must change form, because cobalt and chart blue share a hue family

The conflict is measurable. Cobalt **#002fa7** has a hue of about 223° and a relative luminance of about 0.048. Chart blue **#086CA8** sits at about 202° and 0.136, a contrast ratio of only about **1.9:1**. Against ink #0d0d0d, cobalt manages only about 1.8:1. The AWC's MVFR blue **#0000EE** is worse: hue 240°, luminance about 0.062, a ratio of about **1.1:1** with cobalt. At dot size it is effectively the same colour [E, computed with the WCAG relative-luminance formula].

Two consequences follow. A pilot reads blue on an aero map as "towered" or "Class B/D", so recolouring a selected Class D ring cobalt would read as a change of class. And a cobalt-selected METAR dot next to an MVFR dot would be ambiguous.

The fix is to encode selection by form [E]:

- Keep every chart feature in its chart colour when selected.
- Add a 2 px cobalt **casing ring** offset 2 px outside the symbol or boundary, and raise the feature's own weight by 1 px.
- Dim everything that is not selected, mirroring the `--atc-dimmed` treatment atc already applies to planes.
- Never fill a chart symbol or METAR dot with cobalt.
- Reserve cobalt fills for atc's own objects: planes, chips and focus outlines.

The faithful choice also keeps a little hue distance. #086CA8 is cyan-blue, while #3884C4 from the PDF drifts toward cobalt. If more separation is ever needed, the measured over-terrain teal **#086A8E** (hue ≈196°) is a legitimate chart value pilots see across eastern Oregon [M].

**METAR flight categories** should keep the AWC's dot colours, because pilots know them:

## Facts that overturn the brief's assumptions

The current chart contradicts several premises, and each one changes data or styling.

**Redmond (KRDM) is Class D, not C.** It has a blue dashed ring, a blue towered symbol, "CT - 124.5★" (part-time), ATIS 119.025, a ceiling box reading **56** (5,600 ft MSL), and a "See NOTAMs/Supplement for Class D eff hrs" note. The tower is part-time, so the airspace is too ([Klamath Falls GeoTIFF](https://aeronav.faa.gov/visual/09-03-2026/sectional-files/Klamath_Falls.zip)) [M].

**Bend (KBDN) is non-towered.** It has a magenta symbol, "AWOS-3 134.425", "3459 \*L 52 123.0 Ⓒ" (5,200 ft runway, CTAF/UNICOM 123.0) and "\*RP", inside a **Class E surface area drawn as magenta dashes**. Its runway is now 17/35, not OSM's 16/34 ([NASR APT CSV](https://nfdc.faa.gov/webContent/28DaySub/extra/01_Oct_2026_APT_CSV.zip)) [M; S].

**Medford (KMFR) is charted Class D** on the same chart ([Klamath Falls GeoTIFF](https://aeronav.faa.gov/visual/09-03-2026/sectional-files/Klamath_Falls.zip)) [M]. Only the chart was checked; confirm it against NASR airspace data before encoding.

Among atc's airports, only **KPDX (Class C, magenta screen) and KSEA (Class B, blue screen)** carry the heavy bands ([Seattle GeoTIFF](https://aeronav.faa.gov/visual/09-03-2026/sectional-files/Seattle.zip)) [M]. **The Mode C veil exists only around Class B primaries**, so KSEA has one and KPDX does not ([CUG p. 17](https://aeronav.faa.gov/user_guide/cug-complete_20260709.pdf)) [S].

Five premises about the chart's style also fail:

- **Chart "magenta" is maroon on the product**, not the violet of the user-guide PDF [M vs M-pdf].
- **Class B is a lighter screened blue** than Class D, not the same ink weight [M].
- **Victor airways are bands, not hairlines** [S].
- **VFR waypoints are black, not magenta** [S].
- **Airport diagrams carry no blue or magenta** [S].

The guessed Deschutes VOR position did not fall on its compass rose. Take navaid coordinates from NASR, not from memory [M].

## Conclusion

Faithful sectional symbology turns out to be cheaper than the design worry suggested. The chart's palette is two inks, a handful of screens and black, and atc's ink already is the chart's black. The two places where faithfulness and the LiveLoveApp system collide both resolve in favour of the chart, at no cost. Stepped vignettes replace forbidden gradients, and stepped is what the FAA actually prints. Selection becomes a change of form instead of a change of hue, and that is also the only selection treatment that stays legible next to the AWC's MVFR blue.

The open risks are evidentiary rather than technical. The 2026 spec no longer publishes ink numbers. The vignette's side rests on pilot lore. Symbol sizes are undimensioned. So the measured GeoTIFF values should be the regression baseline. The next useful step is a fixture test that renders KRDM, KBDN and KPDX at z10, compares their stroke colours and dash runs against the measured table, and checks the Class D and E assignments against NASR's airspace classes rather than the chart.
